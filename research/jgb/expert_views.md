# Expert Opinions & Outlook on Japanese Government Bonds (JGBs)
*Research compiled: February 1, 2026*

---

## Current State of Play

**JGB 10-Year Yield:** ~2.25% (as of Jan 30, 2026)
- Hit 27-year high of 2.2%+ in mid-January
- Up 0.18 points over past month
- Up 1.01 points year-over-year
- This is the highest level since 1999

**What's driving this?**
- Fiscal jitters over PM Takaichi's spending plans
- BOJ rate hike expectations (midyear 2026)
- Inflation expectations becoming embedded
- Political uncertainty (snap election Feb 8, 2026)

## The Bearish/Risk Camp

### The "Truss Shock" Scenario

**Sumitomo Mitsui Trust's Inadome (Nov 2025):**
- Warned Japan "not immune to Truss shock risk"
- (Reference to UK's Liz Truss bond meltdown in 2022)
- Predicted 10-year yields could hit 1.8% by year-end 2025
- **Update:** They blew past that — we're at 2.25% now

**The Fiscal Jitters Story:**
- PM Takaichi proposed suspending sales tax on food for 2 years
- Markets freaked out → historic bond selloff in January
- $117bn supplementary budget, 60% funded by new debt
- $74bn in new JGB issuance needed

**Credit Default Swaps (CDS) Market:**
- Default insurance costs hit **2-year high** in December 2025
- This means bond traders are pricing in more risk
- Not panic levels, but notable increase

### The Structural Problems

**Japan is shortening maturities** (Dec 2025):
- Weak demand for ultralong bonds
- Shifting to shorter-term issuance
- Problem: This raises future refinancing risk and interest burden

**Foreign buyers stepping in:**
- For first time ever, overseas buyers acquired **majority of superlong JGBs** (Jan 2026)
- Domestic investors (banks, insurance) are pulling back from ultralong exposure
- Is this good? Depends if you think foreign capital is fickle or stable

**Borrowing costs doubling scenario:**
- If 10-year yield hits 2.5%, Japan's debt service costs could double
- Burden on regional banks
- Though positive for household savings (finally getting yield!)

## Bank of Japan (BOJ) - What's Next?

### What the BOJ Minutes Say (Dec 2025):
- Board members **favor continuing rate hikes**
- Conditional on: "economic activity and price trends evolve in line with forecasts"
- Translation: They want to hike, but they're data-dependent (classic central bank speak)

### Market Expectations:
- **Midyear 2026 rate hike** priced in
- BOJ ended negative rates in 2024, been slowly normalizing since
- 2-year JGB yields already above 1% (Dec 2025) on BOJ hike speculation

### The Gotchas:
- Fragile US economy (Trump policies, global slowdown risks)
- Volatile yen — weakens their ability to hike aggressively
- Political pressure from new government
- BOJ Governor Ueda's speeches being parsed for clues (classic central bank tea-leaf reading)

## Investment Implications

### What Could Go Right (Bull Case):
1. **Yields stabilizing** — fiscal panic was overdone, strong auction demand shows buyers returning
2. **BOJ stays gradual** — no shock hikes, just slow normalization
3. **Inflation moderates** — if wage growth doesn't spiral, yields could ease back to 2%
4. **Political stability** — post-election, clearer fiscal policy reduces uncertainty

### What Could Go Wrong (Bear Case):
1. **Fiscal dominance** — Government spending blows out, bond vigilantes attack (Truss 2.0)
2. **BOJ forced to choose** — hike rates (crash JGBs) or keep rates low (tank yen)
3. **Inflation surprise** — wage-price spiral forces BOJ to hike faster than expected
4. **Foreign buyer reversal** — if overseas investors dump JGBs, who buys them?
5. **Structural debt trap** — Japan's debt-to-GDP already ~260%, highest in developed world

### The Contrarian View:
**Nobody's explicitly saying "buy JGBs" right now**, but the strong auction demand suggests:
- Yields at 2%+ are attractive enough to bring buyers back
- Japan's domestic savings pool is still massive
- Zero alternatives — where else are Japanese pension funds going to put trillions of yen?

**The "carry trade is back" crowd:**
- Borrow yen (still relatively low rates), invest elsewhere
- But this blew up in 2024, so traders are cautious

## Risk Scenarios - What Keeps Bond Traders Up at Night

### Scenario 1: The Spiral
1. BOJ hikes → JGB yields spike → debt service costs explode
2. Government can't afford debt service → more JGB issuance
3. More supply → yields spike further → death spiral
4. BOJ forced to intervene (Yield Curve Control 2.0?)

### Scenario 2: The Yen Crisis
1. BOJ keeps rates low to protect JGBs
2. Yen crashes (interest rate differential with US widens)
3. Imported inflation surges
4. BOJ trapped — can't hike without crashing bonds, can't ease without crashing yen

### Scenario 3: The Goldilocks
1. Fiscal policy moderates post-election
2. BOJ hikes slowly (1-2 more times in 2026)
3. Inflation stays around 2%, stable
4. Yields settle around 2%-2.5% — high enough to attract buyers, low enough to be sustainable

## The Bottom Line (My Take)

**Short-term (Q1-Q2 2026):**
- Yields likely range-bound 2%-2.5%
- Auction demand suggests panic is over
- BOJ probably hikes once more in mid-2026 (barring global crisis)

**Medium-term (2026-2027):**
- All eyes on fiscal policy post-election
- If Takaichi moderates spending → yields ease toward 2%
- If spending blows out → risk of 2.5%+ yields (Truss vibes)

**Long-term (structural):**
- Japan's debt is sustainable... until it's not
- Aging population = shrinking domestic savings pool
- Foreign buyers are fickle — what happens if they leave?
- BOJ is trapped in "low for longer" world, but inflation is forcing their hand

**Contrarian call:**
If you believe:
- Japan won't go full fiscal madness
- BOJ stays cautious
- Global economy avoids recession
Then **JGBs at 2.25% might be a relative value play** compared to other developed market bonds.

But if you're wrong, you're catching a falling knife.
