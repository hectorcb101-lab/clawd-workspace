### Core Concepts from the Paper

**1. Abstracted Power**

Dr Peterson introduces the concept of **"abstracted power"** as a pedagogical tool:

> "We define abstracted power as a human actor's influence or control over a system, process, or dataset which, as a function of the technology that enables it, obscures or distances the human actor from consequences of that influence or control."

**Key insight:** Technology creates emotional distance between developers and the real-world impacts of their decisions. This distance makes it easier to dismiss or ignore consequences.

**2. The Modified Trolley Problem**

She uses a modified version of the Trolley Problem to illustrate abstracted power:
- **Traditional:** Agent pulls a lever directly
- **Modified with intermediation:** Agent writes code at a computer terminal, far removed from the scene
- **Result:** Same outcome, but emotional experience is profoundly different

Students immediately grasp that "the outcome is the same. But the distance from the impact makes all the difference."

**3. Two Key Challenges in CS Ethics Education**

Dr Peterson identifies two critical factors that create abstracted power:

a) **Technological Intermediation**
   - Technologies increasingly allow opacity between developers and users
   - Example: Billions use Google/Meta services developed in Silicon Valley that aim to "gradually" and "imperceptibly" change behaviour thousands of miles away
   - Quote: "This is abstracted power writ large"

b) **Computational Thinking**
   - CS education emphasises reason-centric, abstract problem-solving
   - Students want to believe ethical dilemmas can be resolved through reason alone
   - Like the philosopher Chidi in "The Good Place," they're comfortable with thought experiments but uncomfortable with real consequences

**4. Core Pedagogical Philosophy**

> "Our students often want to believe that ethical dilemmas can be resolved through reason alone... thinking is not enough; [we must] make [students] feel the responsibility they have and the impact their decisions have on others. Only then can [they] meaningfully wrangle with the problem."

**Dr Peterson's approach:** Add emotional engagement to computational thinking. She argues for "increased emotional engagement in computer science ethics education, to encourage students to feel as well as think regarding the potential impacts of their power on others."

### Four Pedagogical Approaches (from the paper)

The paper proposes four concrete approaches to enable emotional engagement:
1. Making consequences visceral and immediate
2. Encouraging empathy through perspective-taking
3. Using real-world case studies with human impact
4. Creating friction in computational thinking processes

## Key Themes for Student Work

If writing for Dr Peterson, students should consider:

1. **Don't treat ethics as purely rational/abstract**
   - Show awareness of emotional dimensions
   - Acknowledge the human impacts, not just theoretical frameworks

2. **Examine power dynamics**
   - Who has power in the technological system?
   - Who is affected by that power?
   - What intermediaries create distance?

3. **Question technological determinism**
   - Technology isn't neutral
   - Designers make choices that have moral implications
   - Consider alternatives and whose interests are served

4. **Bridge theory and practice**
   - Real-world examples matter
   - Connect abstract principles to concrete human experiences
   - Show empathy for those affected by technology

5. **Acknowledge complexity and uncertainty**
   - Don't oversimplify ethical dilemmas
   - Recognise that "thinking through" isn't always enough
   - Be humble about the limits of prediction and control

6. **Consider historical and cultural context**
   - Her critical theory background suggests she values contextual analysis
   - Power inequalities have history
   - Cultural assumptions shape technological development

## Summary for Students

Dr Theodora Petkova Peterson is an ethics educator who brings a unique humanistic perspective to computer science. Her core intellectual concern is that technology creates **emotional distance** between developers and the real human consequences of their work—what she calls "abstracted power." 

She believes that ethical reasoning in CS cannot be purely intellectual; students must **feel** their responsibility, not just think about it. Her background in journalism, critical theory, and media studies informs a teaching approach that values:
- Emotional engagement with ethics
- Critical examination of power structures
- Interdisciplinary thinking
- Real human impacts over abstract principles
- Social justice and historical context

When writing for her, demonstrate that you understand technology isn't neutral, that ethical decisions have human stakes, and that being a good technologist requires both head and heart.
