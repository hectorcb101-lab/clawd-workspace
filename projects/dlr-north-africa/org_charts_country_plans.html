Dar As-Sikkah — State Printing Works

Location: Rabat, Morocco · Established: 1987 · Role: Sole domestic printer of Moroccan banknotes and coins

Morocco is fully self-sufficient in banknote printing via Dar As-Sikkah. DLR opportunity is in substrate, security features, ink, and equipment rather than finished banknotes.

Note Life & Circulation

  • Highest denomination: 200 MAD (~$20 USD)
  • Most used: 20 and 50 MAD for daily transactions
  • New 2023 series: Unveiled 24 November 2023 — new banknotes and coins including redesigned 100 MAD
  • Substrate: Paper (cotton-based) — Durasafe® hybrid used for commemorative only
  • Exchange rate peg: 60% EUR / 40% USD basket with ±5% band
  • Currency NOT freely convertible on international markets

DLR Opportunity Assessment

Opportunities:
  • Substrate supply to Dar As-Sikkah (paper, security thread, foils)
  • Security feature technology (holograms, inks, threads) for new series
  • Equipment/press supply and maintenance
  • Consultancy on new denomination designs
  • Polymer/hybrid substrate proposal for future series
Challenges:
  • Fully self-sufficient domestic printer — no finished banknote opportunity
  • Dar As-Sikkah has 37+ years of operational experience
  • Political sensitivity around currency sovereignty
  • Long-standing Governor (23 years) — established supplier relationships

Key Currency Intelligence

  • Highest denomination: 2,000 DA (~$15 USD at official rate, ~$8.40 at parallel rate)
  • Frequently used: 500, 1000, 2000 DA
  • 100 DA note being replaced by coins
  • Massive cash economy: ~50% of currency circulates outside the banking system
  • Parallel FX market: Official 134 DZD/USD vs parallel ~238 DZD/USD (78% premium)
  • Active modernisation: New 500, 1000, and multiple 2000 DA designs issued 2018-2022
  • Domestic printing: Bank of Algeria has own printing works but capacity may be limited for volume
  • Historical link to Banque de France for banknote production (post-independence)

DLR Opportunity Assessment

🔥 HIGH PRIORITY — Opportunities:
  • Volume: 45.6M population × heavy cash economy = massive banknote demand
  • New governor (Jan 2026): Fresh leadership = window to establish new supplier relationships
  • Active programme: Multiple new denominations 2018-2022 shows appetite for modernisation
  • Security upgrades: Progression from basic → holographic → enhanced security features
  • Substrate & components: Paper, security thread, holograms, ink supply
  • Finished banknote supply: If domestic capacity is insufficient for demand
  • Strong FX reserves ($64.6B): Algeria can afford premium products
Challenges:
  • Political instability — 5 governors in 7 years
  • Complex procurement processes (state-controlled)
  • Franco-Algerian historical ties may favour French suppliers
  • Opaque decision-making (limited public procurement data)

Key Currency Intelligence

  • Closed currency: Illegal to import or export TND — all exchange within Tunisia
  • Only 4 denominations currently in circulation
  • 1 TND ≈ $0.32 USD
  • Paper substrate with protective varnish coat (not polymer)
  • Complete series upgrade 2017-2022: All 4 denominations redesigned over 5 years
  • Crane Currency is incumbent: RAPID® HD is their proprietary security thread
  • De La Rue historical link: Printed 1972 series (½, 1, and 5 Dinars)
  • Severe fiscal pressure: Budget deficit 6% GDP, Moody's Caa1, limited FX reserves
